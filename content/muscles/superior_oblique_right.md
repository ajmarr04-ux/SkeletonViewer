Superior oblique arises from the body of the sphenoid above and medial to the optic canal and runs forwards along the superomedial wall of the orbit. Its tendon passes through the trochlea, a fibrocartilaginous pulley near the orbital margin, then turns back laterally to the sclera. It depresses the eye, mainly when it is adducted, and also abducts and intorts it.
