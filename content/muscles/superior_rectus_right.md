Superior rectus runs forwards above the eyeball, from the common tendinous ring at the apex of the orbit to the upper surface of the sclera. Because the orbit points laterally, its pull is not straight up: it elevates the eye, especially when the eye looks outwards, and also adducts it and turns its upper pole medially (intorsion).
