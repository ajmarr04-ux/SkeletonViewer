## Articulations (`articulations.json`)

Drafted in Milestone 4, session J, from standard descriptions (Gray's Anatomy style), not checked line by line against a reference. 357 pairs.

### Left out (not sure they count, or they're variable)

1. **Cuboid–navicular.** Gray's says the cuboid "occasionally" articulates with the navicular (often only a fibrous union). Left out.
2. **Base of metatarsal 1–metatarsal 2.** Occasionally a small facet. Left out; intermetatarsal pairs 2–3, 3–4 and 4–5 are in.
3. **Metacarpal 1–metacarpal 2.** No intermetacarpal joint is usually described for the thumb. Left out; 2–3, 3–4 and 4–5 are in.
4. **Sphenoid–maxilla.** Sometimes described at the maxillary tuberosity. Left out.
5. **Rib 10–T9.** Rib 10's head usually has a single facet for T10, sometimes also T9. Only T10 recorded.
6. **Ulna–triquetrum.** The ulna is separated from the carpus by the articular disc of the distal radio-ulnar joint. Left out; the radiocarpal joint is recorded as radius–scaphoid and radius–lunate.
7. **Interchondral joints (costal cartilages 6 to 9) and rib–rib contact.** Cartilage to cartilage, not bone to bone, and cartilage isn't in the model. Left out.
8. **Clavicle–rib 1.** Joined by the costoclavicular ligament, not a joint. Left out (rib 1 is in the clavicle's region set instead).
9. **Hyoid.** No bony articulation (spec §9 allowlist).

### Recorded, but please check

10. **Sternocostal joints go through the costal cartilage**, which the model leaves out. Ribs 1 to 7 are recorded as articulating with the sternum ("through costal cartilage"); ribs 8 to 10 (which reach it only through the cartilage above) and 11 and 12 (floating) aren't. Is that the right rule for the app?
11. **Stapes–temporal bone: tympanostapedial syndesmosis** (the footplate in the oval window, held by the annular ligament). Recorded as an articulation so the stapes shows its housing at tier 1. The malleus and incus are attached to the temporal bone by ligaments only, so they aren't paired with it.
12. **Lunate–hamate.** Recorded (Gray's lists five bones for the lunate, including the hamate), although a lunate–hamate facet is absent in some people.
13. **Lateral cuneiform–metatarsal 4.** Recorded (Gray's lists the 2nd, 3rd and 4th metatarsals).
14. **Spheno-occipital synchondrosis.** Fused in the adult; kept as the sphenoid–occipital contact.
15. **Joints named here without a standard Latin name** use a plain description with an en dash, for example "Sphenoid–palatine articulation", "Ethmoid–nasal articulation", "Vomer–maxilla articulation", "Ethmoid–inferior nasal concha articulation". Replace with the reference names if wanted.
16. **One entry per pair:** where two bones meet at more than one joint, the pair is stored once with a combined name: radius–ulna ("Proximal and distal radio-ulnar joints"), tibia–fibula ("Tibiofibular joints"), and a rib with its own vertebra ("Joint of head and costotransverse joint").

99. **Radial and ulnar deviation of the wrist** are written as `abduction` and `adduction` at the *wrist* functional joint, as plan decision 7 foresaw: flexor carpi radialis and extensor carpi radialis longus (abduction, `prime`), extensor carpi radialis brevis and abductor pollicis longus (`assist`); flexor and extensor carpi ulnaris (adduction, `prime`). 100. **Thumb and little-finger movements.** Thumb actions name the single pairs: carpometacarpal (trapezium–first metacarpal), metacarpophalangeal 1 and interphalangeal 1. Abduction and adduction of the thumb mean movement away from and back to the palm (the anatomical convention). Opposition is `prime` for opponens pollicis and `assist` for abductor and flexor pollicis brevis. Opponens digiti minimi is given `opposition` at the hamate–fifth metacarpal joint, stretching the word to cover the little finger's half of opposition; the alternative is `flexion` (`assist`) there, or `functions` only.
101. **Innervation ranges that differ between texts:** supinator C5–C6 (Gray's; others C6–C7 or C7–C8); brachioradialis C5–C6 (some C5–C7); brachialis musculocutaneous with a small radial part (C7); flexor pollicis brevis deep head ulnar (it is median in some people); the lumbricals' split (lateral two median, medial two ulnar) varies; flexor carpi ulnaris C7–T1; palmaris longus C7–C8; pronator teres C6–C7.

Flexor digitorum superficialis stays in the *superficial* anterior group, as the source has it, though many texts call it the intermediate layer. Brachioradialis and anconeus are in the posterior superficial forearm, as in the source and Moore.
103. **Palmaris longus** is modelled as the source has it: a muscle with no mesh of its own, a belly, and the palmar aponeurosis as its aponeurosis part. The aponeurosis's origin is `soft` ("tendon of palmaris longus and the flexor retinaculum"), since the palmar aponeurosis is present even when palmaris longus is absent; it inserts on the deep transverse metacarpal ligament (a ligament-layer object) and `soft` skin and fibrous flexor sheaths.
104. **Grouped hand muscles.** The source has the four dorsal interossei, the three palmar interossei and the four lumbricals each as one object, so each file merges its slips' attachments and acts on the MCP, PIP and DIP *2–5* functional joints, though each slip acts on particular fingers (for example the dorsal interossei abduct only the index, middle and ring fingers). The thumb's palmar interosseous, counted by some texts as a fourth, is not described. The lumbricals' origin is the `flexor_digitorum_profundus` muscle, so the profundus appears in their derived attachments as a structure.
105. **Insertions written as the textbooks do, not through the modelled ligament.** Flexor carpi ulnaris inserts on the pisiform, the hook of the hamate and the base of the fifth metacarpal (the last two through the pisohamate and pisometacarpal ligaments, which are ligament-layer objects); the long extensors' extensor expansions are written as the bases of the middle and distal phalanges. These add bone links the old `muscleAttachments` lists didn't have (flexor carpi ulnaris on the hamate and fifth metacarpal, flexor carpi radialis on the third metacarpal, the deep head of flexor pollicis brevis on the trapezoid and capitate, abductor pollicis brevis on the scaphoid and trapezium). The two-way check accepts extras, and every old string on an upper-limb bone is matched (preview: 0 misses).
106. **Landmarks added** (names only, `position: null`): humerus (anterior surface, medial border, lateral and medial supracondylar ridges), radius (anterior oblique line, pronator tuberosity, anterior, posterior and lateral surfaces), ulna (supinator crest, anterior, medial and posterior surfaces, posterior border). Without finer landmarks, the clavicular part of deltoid uses the clavicle's *acromial end* for "lateral third", and supraspinatus, infraspinatus and teres minor all use *greater tubercle* (no facets).
107. **Actions with a judgement in them.** The long head of biceps is given shoulder `stabilisation` only (its abduction with the arm laterally rotated is left out); the deltoid's parts inherit the muscle's abduction, so the spinal part counts as an abductor and a synergist of supraspinatus; anconeus extends the elbow (`assist`) and steadies it; pronator quadratus also steadies the distal radio-ulnar joint; teres minor and subscapularis assist adduction; extensor digitorum is `prime` at the knuckles and `assist` at the finger joints; the lumbricals are `prime` for MCP flexion and PIP extension, `assist` for DIP extension.

## Muscles: head and neck (Milestone 8, session Y)

82 left and midline files (63 muscles, 19 parts): scalp, face, extra-ocular, mastication, tongue, soft palate, neck (superficial, suprahyoid, infrahyoid, prevertebral, scalenes, pharynx, larynx) and the suboccipital group. The calls worth checking:

78. **Rotation has no direction.** The vocabulary has one `rotation`, so the left and right sternocleidomastoids (which turn the face to opposite sides) count as synergists for rotation, and so do the obliquus capitis inferior of one side and the sternocleidomastoid of the other, which really do work together. The same will apply to every rotator of the neck and trunk in session Z. Options: leave it (the default), or add `rotation_ipsilateral` / `rotation_contralateral` to `movements.json` (a vocabulary change, so the user's call).
79. **Muscles with `actions: []`** (plan decision 7), described by `functions` instead: the scalp, face, eye, tongue, soft palate, pharynx, larynx and infrahyoid muscles, platysma and stylohyoid (no joint in `articulations.json` for the eye or the hyoid). The suprahyoid muscles that open the mouth (digastric, mylohyoid, geniohyoid) do have an action: the temporomandibular joint, `depression`, `assist`. Side-to-side grinding (pterygoids, masseter) has no movement in the vocabulary, so it is in `functions` only.
80. **Rib elevation.** Scalenus anterior and medius `elevation` of the first rib at `costovertebral_joint_rib_1_t1`; scalenus posterior at `costovertebral_joint_rib_2_t2` (the second rib also meets T1; one pair was chosen). Session Z added a functional joint, *costovertebral joints* (all the rib–vertebra pairs of one side), for the muscles that move many ribs at once (intercostals, levatores costarum, serratus posterior, transversus thoracis); because it overlaps the single pairs, the scalenes and the external intercostals still meet as synergists.
81. **Innervation ranges that differ between texts:** scalenus anterior C4–C6, medius C3–C8, posterior C6–C8; longus capitis C1–C3, longus colli C2–C6; sternocleidomastoid accessory nerve with sensory C2–C3.
82. **Names and Latin.** File names follow the source (`rectus_anterior_capitis`, `obliquus_inferior_capitis`, `levator_nasolabialis`), with the TA2 Latin (musculus rectus capitis anterior, obliquus capitis inferior, levator labii superioris alaeque nasi). Two parts exist only in the source, so their Latin is formed by analogy and is not a TA2 term: *Venter musculi recti superioris* (belly of superior rectus) and *Pars externa musculi thyroarytenoidei* (external part of thyro-arytenoid).
83. **The larynx and pharynx attach mostly to cartilages that aren't modelled** (thyroid, cricoid, arytenoid, epiglottis; laryngeal cartilage is excluded, spec §12), so their attachments are `soft` text, and these muscles have no derived bone links apart from the hyoid, sphenoid, mandible, occipital and temporal attachments of the constrictors and stylopharyngeus.
84. **Digastric** is modelled as written in the source: both bellies insert into the intermediate tendon (a part), and the tendon inserts on the body and greater horn of the hyoid. So the hyoid's derived insertions list the *tendon*, matching the old "Digastric (through a fibrous loop)".
85. **Lateral pterygoid:** protraction `prime`, depression `assist` on the muscle; the superior head adds `stabilisation` (`stabiliser`) of the temporomandibular joint, for its pull on the articular disc during closing, and inserts on the articular disc (a cartilage-layer object) as well as the pterygoid fovea. Masseter's superficial part adds protraction (`assist`), its deep part retraction (`assist`).
86. **Sternocleidomastoid actions:** lateral flexion and flexion of the cervical spine and rotation at the craniovertebral joints (`prime`), extension of the head at the atlanto-occipital joint (`assist`, both muscles acting with the neck flexed forwards). Some texts give extension a larger role.
87. **Landmarks added to bone files** (names only, `position: null`): mandible (oblique line, digastric fossa, superior and inferior mental spines, incisive fossa, pterygoid fovea), maxilla (infra-orbital margin, canine fossa, incisive fossa), temporal bone (mastoid notch), sphenoid (infratemporal crest, pterygoid hamulus), occipital bone (pharyngeal tubercle, jugular process), axis (transverse process). Attachments without a suitable landmark name the bone only (for example, inferior oblique on the maxilla's `body`, for the orbital floor).
88. **Variable muscles described as such:** temporoparietalis and risorius (often weak or absent). Rectus capitis posterior minor's connection to the spinal dura is mentioned as "may help keep the dura from folding".

## Muscles: back, thorax, abdomen and pelvis (Milestone 8, session Z)

59 left and midline files (50 muscles, 9 parts; 57 mirrored; the diaphragm and the linea alba are midline). The calls worth checking:

89. **Trunk movements name the thoracic and lumbar spine separately**, not *vertebral column*, so that the abdominal muscles and erector spinae don't become synergists of the neck flexors and extensors through the nested spine entry (QUESTIONS 72). Rotation still has no direction (QUESTIONS 78): external oblique (turns the trunk to the opposite side) and internal oblique (same side) of one side are listed as synergists.
90. **Scapular movements** act at the *shoulder girdle* (sternoclavicular + acromioclavicular): trapezius (upward rotation `prime`, retraction `assist` for the whole muscle; its parts add elevation, retraction and depression as `prime`), serratus anterior (protraction, upward rotation), rhomboids (retraction, downward rotation; elevation `assist`), levator scapulae (elevation; downward rotation `assist`), pectoralis minor (depression; protraction and downward rotation `assist`), subclavius (`stabilisation`; depression `assist`).
91. **Respiratory muscles.** External intercostals and levatores costarum raise the ribs; internal and innermost intercostals and transversus thoracis lower them; serratus posterior superior and inferior are given elevation and depression as `assist` only, with a note that their respiratory role is doubtful. The diaphragm has `actions: []` and `functions` (no joint).

These are exactly the old strings marked `indirect` (QUESTIONS 76); every other old string on the trunk bones is matched (285).
93. **Spans given as textbook ranges, which vary between people and books:** iliocostalis lumborum to ribs 6–12, iliocostalis thoracis from ribs 7–12 to ribs 1–6 and C7, longissimus thoracis to all thoracic transverse processes and ribs 3–12, spinalis thoracis T11–L2 to T1–T8, semispinalis thoracis T6–T10 to C6–T4, semispinalis colli T1–T6 to C2–C5, multifidus spanning two to four segments, rotatores on T1–T12, levatores longi only from T7–T10. Interspinales thoracis are given only at T1–T2 and T11–T12.
94. **Spinalis colli** is described as inconstant, and **spinalis capitis** as usually inseparable from semispinalis capitis (which the source doesn't have, QUESTIONS 70). **Pyramidalis** is "often absent". The **abdominal part of pectoralis major** is flagged inconstant in the source.
95. **Lateral intertransversarii lumborum** are given anterior-ramus innervation (L1–L4) for both parts, as hypaxial muscles; some texts give the dorsal parts posterior rami. The source's dorsal parts are described as running from a transverse process to the accessory process above.
96. **Levator ani** has no muscle of its own in the source: pubococcygeus, pubo-analis and iliococcygeus are separate muscles (and puborectalis isn't modelled). Their attachments use the pubic crest for "the back of the body of the pubis", for want of a better landmark, and `soft` text for the tendinous arch.
97. **Landmarks added:** humerus (crest of the greater tubercle, crest of the lesser tubercle) and hip bone (pubic crest). Ribs 1, 11 and 12 have no *angle* landmark, so attachments there name the rib only.
98. **Linea alba** (a part without a muscle): from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis, given as the interpubic disc (a cartilage-layer object) plus `soft` "pubic symphysis".

## Muscles: lower limb (Milestone 8, session AB)

67 left files (52 muscles, 15 parts; 67 mirrored): iliopsoas (2), gluteal superficial (4, with the tensor's belly and the iliotibial tract) and deep (5), the thigh (anterior 6, medial 6, posterior 3), the leg (anterior 4, lateral 2, posterior superficial 3, posterior deep 4) and the foot (dorsum 2, sole 11). The calls worth checking:

108. **Hierarchy.** Psoas major and iliacus sit under *Lower limb › Hip › Iliopsoas*, as the source has them (some texts put psoas with the posterior abdominal wall). Obturator externus stays in the *medial compartment of the thigh*, as in the source and Moore, not with the short lateral rotators. Extensor hallucis brevis has its own file because the source has it as a separate object, though many texts call it the medial slip of extensor digitorum brevis. All the plantar muscles and both interossei are under one *Sole of the foot* compartment (the source doesn't split the four layers).
109. **The calcaneal tendon** is a part of gastrocnemius (`alsoServes: [soleus]`), as the source has it. Gastrocnemius and soleus both insert on the *tendon*, and the tendon inserts on the calcaneal tuberosity, as digastric was handled (question 84). So the calcaneus's derived insertions list the tendon, not soleus, and session AD's two-way check should count the old "Soleus" string on the calcaneus as matched through the tendon's `alsoServes`. (The iliotibial tract is handled the same way for gluteus maximus, but gluteus maximus also inserts directly on the gluteal tuberosity.)
110. **Quadriceps on the tibia.** The four quadriceps muscles insert on the base of the patella and on the patellar ligament (a ligament-layer object), not on the tibia, so the old "Quadriceps femoris (through the patellar ligament)" string on the tibia stays one of the `indirect` ones (question 76).
111. **Innervation ranges that differ between texts:** psoas major L1–L3 (some L1–L4); pectineus femoral nerve L2–L3 with an inconstant obturator or accessory obturator branch; gluteus maximus L5–S2; the deep gluteal muscles L5–S1 (piriformis S1–S2); extensor digitorum brevis and extensor hallucis brevis L5–S1 (some S1–S2); flexor digitorum longus and flexor hallucis longus S2–S3 (Moore; others L5–S2); popliteus L4–S1; the intrinsic foot muscles S1–S3 split between the medial and lateral plantar nerves as Gray's gives them.
112. **Actions with a judgement in them.** Gluteus maximus is given knee `stabilisation` (through the iliotibial tract) and abduction only as `assist` (its upper fibres); gluteus medius' medial rotation is `assist` (front fibres), so it counts as an antagonist of the lateral rotators; sartorius gets five `assist` actions; biceps femoris is given lateral rotation of the knee as `prime` and the medial hamstrings medial rotation as `assist`; popliteus medial rotation of the knee as `prime` (the "unlocking" of the knee is usually described as rotation of the femur on the tibia; with the tibia free it rotates the tibia medially); vastus medialis gets patellofemoral `stabilisation`. Psoas major's lateral flexion of the lumbar spine makes it a synergist of the trunk side-benders of both sides, as lateral flexion has no direction (the same issue as rotation, question 78).
113. **Foot movements.** Inversion and eversion act at the *subtalar and transverse tarsal joints* functional joint; dorsiflexion and plantarflexion at the *ankle* (talocrural). Tibialis anterior and posterior invert (`prime`), the long toe flexors and extensor hallucis longus help (`assist`); fibularis longus and brevis evert (`prime`), extensor digitorum longus and fibularis tertius help. The big toe uses the single pairs (metatarsophalangeal 1, interphalangeal 1) and the little toe's own muscles the fifth metatarsophalangeal pair, which overlaps the *2–5* set, so abductor digiti minimi counts as a synergist of the dorsal interossei (as in the hand, question 104).
114. **Grouped foot muscles** (as in the hand, question 104): the four dorsal interossei, three plantar interossei and four lumbricals are one object each, so their attachments are merged and they act on the *2–5* functional joints. The dorsal interossei abduct from the line of the **second** toe. Extensor digitorum brevis is written as inserting on the middle and distal phalanges of toes 2–4 through the long extensor tendons' expansions (the old bone lists say the same), with a `soft` line for the tendons themselves.
115. **Opponens digiti minimi of the foot** is in the source as a separate object but is inconstant and not in every text (Gray's mentions it as deep fibres of flexor digiti minimi brevis). It has `actions: []` and a cautious function ("may draw the fifth metatarsal down and in"), since no listed movement fits and there's no metatarsal joint in the vocabulary that would carry it honestly.
116. **Adductor magnus** has no mesh of its own: the source splits it into *adductor minimus* and a *main part*. The main part carries the hamstring (ischiocondylar) part as well as the rest of the adductor fibres, so it gets the hip `extension` action and the mixed innervation; adductor minimus only adducts (from the muscle).
117. **Articularis genus** has no mesh in the source and no parts. It has `actions: []` and a function (drawing up the suprapatellar bursa), and its insertion is `soft`, since the bursa and synovial membrane aren't objects.
118. **Common names** used: "Calf muscle" (gastrocnemius), "Achilles tendon" (calcaneal tendon), "IT band" (iliotibial tract), "Peroneus longus" and "Peroneus brevis" (the older names, still in wide clinical use) and "Flexor accessorius" (quadratus plantae). Say if any of these should be dropped.
119. **Landmarks added** (names only, `position: null`): hip bone (gluteal surface, body of the pubis, superior and inferior pubic rami, ischial ramus), femur (trochanteric fossa, quadrate tubercle, pectineal line, anterior and lateral surfaces, medial and lateral supracondylar lines, popliteal surface), tibia (medial, lateral and posterior surfaces), fibula (medial, lateral and posterior surfaces). Gerdy's tubercle (the iliotibial tract's insertion) is written as the tibia's *lateral condyle*; the obturator muscles' bony origins as the *obturator foramen* (its margin).

## Origins, insertions and antagonists (Milestone 9, session AG)

132. **Attachment patches against the content** (plan decision 6; `assets/build/REPORT-patches.md`, rebuilt by `npm run content:patch-check`). The content decides what is drawn: of 697 patches, 512 are drawn for at least one muscle or part and **185 are never drawn** because no structure they serve names their bone in their role (91 on the left and midline, listed in the report with what the content says). Plan decision 7 shows them as synergists, never in the antagonist tint, so "Show antagonists" shows only muscles that are antagonists *and not* synergists.
