The posterior cruciate ligament lies inside the fibrous capsule of the knee but outside its synovial cavity. It is stronger than the anterior cruciate ligament. It runs from the posterior intercondylar area of the tibia upwards, forwards and medially to the lateral surface of the medial femoral condyle. It stops the tibia sliding backwards on the femur and helps to prevent hyperflexion.
