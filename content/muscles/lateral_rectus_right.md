Lateral rectus arises from the lateral part of the common tendinous ring, bridging the superior orbital fissure, and runs forwards along the lateral wall of the orbit to the lateral surface of the sclera. It is the only muscle that turns the eye outwards without vertical or torsional effects, and the only extra-ocular muscle supplied by the abducens nerve.
