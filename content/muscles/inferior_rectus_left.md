Inferior rectus runs forwards below the eyeball, from the lower part of the common tendinous ring to the inferior surface of the sclera. Like superior rectus it pulls at an angle to the visual axis: it depresses the eye, especially when it looks outwards, and also adducts it and turns its upper pole laterally (extorsion).
